Schema Analysis:
  - Tables joined   : billing, encounters, providers, specialties
  - Number of joins : 3 (all INNER) - the longest chain in the lab, exactly the
                      billing -> encounters -> providers -> specialties path the
                      brief names
  - Rows scanned    : 126,649  (56,574 billing rows surviving the filter, from
                      61,598, + 70,004 encounters + 63 + 8)
  - Rows aggregated : 56,574 -> 192 output rows

Bottleneck Identified:
  The same disease as Q1, one hop worse, but the mildest case in the lab.

  Revenue lives in billing, the month lives on encounters, and the specialty is
  two further hops away through providers. Nothing in the schema lets a claim
  know which specialty earned it, so the whole chain must be walked to attach one
  label to each of 56,574 rows. The plan builds a 70,004-row hash of encounters
  and streams billing past it, then hashes the two small dimensions.

  It is only ~93ms because the aggregation is a HashAggregate rather than the
  sort Q1 needs - SUM and COUNT can both run with a running accumulator, so
  nothing has to be ordered. That is the useful contrast with Q1: same shape,
  one more join, but no COUNT(DISTINCT), and it comes out twice as fast.

  What makes it slow is therefore not the aggregate but the join chain plus
  re-deriving date_trunc('month', ...) on every surviving row, all of it
  recomputed from scratch on every run.

  Four decisions this query forced, all recorded in design_decisions.txt:
  a) Month comes from encounter_date, not claim_date, so revenue lines up with
     when care was delivered rather than when paperwork was filed weeks later.
  b) allowed_amount (what the insurer agreed to pay), not claim_amount (what was
     asked for). The brief asks for allowed.
  c) Denied claims are excluded - 5,024 of 61,598. Counting them overstates
     revenue at $151.8M against a truthful $139.6M. Pending claims are kept,
     because allowed_amount is the contractually agreed figure.
  d) The query is anchored FROM billing, so the 8,406 unbilled encounters simply
     do not appear. That is right for a revenue question - there is no
     allowed_amount to sum. It would be wrong for "average revenue per
     encounter", which is a different question needing a LEFT JOIN and COALESCE.
