Comparison:
  - OLTP  : 8,701.076 ms   (sql/analysis/q3_readmission.sql)
  - STAR  :     5.889 ms
  - Improvement: 1,477x faster
  - Parity: identical (8 rows, both EXCEPT directions, tests/12_parity_q3.sql), and
            the flag itself proven row-by-row against the OLTP self-join across all
            70,004 encounters (tests/14_readmission_flag.sql)

Why it is faster:
  The self-join was not optimised. It stopped existing.

  is_readmit_30d is a boolean computed once during the ETL, so the question becomes
  a GROUP BY over a boolean, served by the partial index idx_fact_inpatient, which
  covers only the ~12% of rows that are inpatient stays.

  The buffers make the point better than the milliseconds:

      OLTP : 8,939,200 buffer hits   (~71 GB of logical reads, for 8 rows)
      STAR :     2,018 buffer hits
      ratio: 4,430x fewer pages touched

  Q1, Q2 and Q4 got 1.4-3.1x because their work was made cheaper. Q3 got 1,477x
  because its work moved to load time and is not done at query time at all. Those
  are different kinds of win; averaging them would misrepresent both.

The cost, stated honestly - is_readmit_30d is DERIVED data:
  1. If the definition changes from 30 to 45 days, the column must be RELOADED. No
     query edit fixes it. days_to_next_encounter sits beside it so the threshold
     stays auditable without returning to the source.
  2. It depends on the FUTURE. A stay discharged Monday only becomes a readmission
     when the return arrives, so incremental loads must recompute already-loaded
     rows at least 30 days back.
  3. It can silently drift from the source definition. That is why tests/14
     compares the flag row-by-row rather than trusting the 8 aggregated rows in
     tests/12, where two opposite errors could cancel out. A fast, confident, wrong
     answer is the failure mode this design invites.

On the cohort translation:
  Q3's index stays are encounter_type = 'Inpatient' AND discharge_date IS NOT NULL.
  The star equivalent is f.is_inpatient AND f.discharge_date_key <> -1, because the
  ETL maps a NULL discharge to the Unknown date member. That second predicate
  matches 0 rows today, but without it this stops being a translation of Q3 the
  moment one open stay exists - and it would fail silently.
