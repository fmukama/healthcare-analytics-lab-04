Comparison:
  - OLTP  : 101.355 ms   (sql/analysis/q1_monthly_encounters.sql)
  - STAR  :  71.254 ms
  - Improvement: 1.4x faster
  - Parity: identical, both EXCEPT directions, 576 rows (tests/10_parity_q1.sql)

Why it is faster:
  1. One hop instead of a chain. specialty_key sits on the fact, so
     encounters -> providers -> specialties collapses and dim_provider is never
     touched. The remaining joins are INDEPENDENT: the planner hashes three small
     dimensions and streams the fact past them once.
  2. The month is a stored column - no date_trunc() per row.
  3. The sort almost disappears: 70,004 rows in 6,875 kB becomes 576 rows in 64 kB.

The honest part - the naive rewrite was SLOWER than the original:
  The obvious translation measured 241ms against the OLTP's 151ms in the same
  session. Two causes, neither visible in a schema diagram:
  - fact_encounters is 16 MB where encounters is 5 MB. The fact carries eleven
    measures the source does not, so it is 3x the pages to scan. A star schema is
    not automatically smaller; here it is deliberately bigger.
  - Postgres has no hash path for COUNT(DISTINCT), so the one-level form still
    sorted all 70,004 rows, now off a wider table.

  Two fixes, both dimensional-modelling technique rather than tuning:
  - COUNT(DISTINCT patient) became a TWO-LEVEL aggregation, so the planner
    HashAggregates twice instead of sorting once.
  - The text-carrying dimensions are joined AFTER the aggregate, so GROUP BY runs
    on 4-byte keys instead of VARCHAR(100). This is the concrete reason surrogate
    keys are narrow integers, and here it is worth more than the removed join.

  Buffers went UP, 653 to 2,023, and the query still got faster. Fewer pages read
  is not what did it; not sorting 70,004 rows is.

  Smallest speedup of the four, and worth stating plainly: Q1's bottleneck was a
  sort, and a sort can only be made cheaper, never abolished.
